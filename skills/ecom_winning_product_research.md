# Skill: E-commerce Winning Product Research

Source basis: user-provided “SUPREME ECOM PRODUCTS August 2026” research sheet. This is a reusable research heuristic, not a guarantee that a product will sell.

## Goal
Evaluate candidate e-commerce / dropshipping products consistently before investing time in a Shopify launch.

## Validation gate
A candidate is stronger when it:
- solves a clear problem or has a strong wow factor;
- can plausibly sell around $30–$120;
- has a low barrier to entry and a mass-market audience;
- shows recent ad engagement (the source asks for activity in roughly the last 48 hours–7 days, and the stricter winning-product section asks that the listed ad’s latest engagement be within about a day / ideally hours);
- has roughly 500+ combined comments/shares/likes on the referenced ad;
- supports at least about 2.5x selling-price-to-COGS economics;
- reveals unmet wants or objections in competitor ad comments that can become a differentiated angle;
- has a credible way to compete rather than merely copy.

## Winning-product evidence checklist
1. Price: target sale price >= $30 where practical and >=2.5x COGS multiplier.
2. Supplier: look for a credible supplier listing (examples in source: AutoDS, AliExpress, CJDropshipping/TeemDrop) with meaningful proof of demand; source benchmark is 500+ sold.
3. Competitor store: inspect a well-made Shopify competitor selling near the intended price.
4. Competitor ads: seek more than 3 but fewer than 25 unique advertisers/competitors and verify recent engagement.
5. Differentiation: mine comments, reviews and creative for pain points, desired features, objections and audience language.
6. Creative: produce at least three materially different ad angles tied to different pain points, audiences or motivations.

## Store audit rubric
Positive signals include sticky add-to-cart, currency conversion where useful, high-quality images/GIFs/video, fast pages, sensible pop-ups, upsells, bulk discounts, coherent branding, shipping expectation icons, target-audience imagery, clear “how to use”, features and benefits.
Negative signals are the inverse: missing purchase aids, weak/too little/too much media, slow or busy pages, bad pop-ups, no upsells or bulk incentives.

## Output Atlas should produce
For each researched product return:
- product + category;
- supplier/COGS evidence;
- target selling price;
- gross dollar spread and selling-price/COGS multiplier;
- problem/wow-factor score;
- market breadth;
- competitor count and freshness of ad engagement;
- competitor-store strengths/weaknesses;
- comment/review insights and differentiation opportunity;
- 3 distinct ad angles;
- risks (seasonality, compliance/health claims, saturation, shipping, quality, returns);
- verdict: Reject / Watch / Test, with reasons and what evidence is still missing.

## Important reasoning rules
- Treat the PDF’s product list and metrics as a dated snapshot, not evergreen truth. Revalidate supplier cost, sales, competitor count and ad engagement before acting.
- Do not confuse gross spread with net profit. Advertising, payment fees, refunds, shipping, taxes and support are not included in the simple selling-price minus COGS figures.
- Do not invent sales, engagement, reviews or health outcomes.
- Health/beauty/wellness products require extra claim/compliance scrutiny.
- A product passing the checklist is a test candidate, not a guaranteed winner.
